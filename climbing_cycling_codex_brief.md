# Codex implementation brief: 3-week climbing + cycling training plan

## Task
Add a responsive **Training Plan** page/feature to my existing project website using the project's current framework, styling conventions, and components. Inspect the repository first, then implement the plan below as editable, structured data (for example, a TypeScript object or JSON) rather than hardcoding every session directly into UI markup. Do not introduce a new framework or unnecessary dependencies.

The plan runs **Monday 19 October through Sunday 8 November 2026** in the **Australia/Perth** time zone. Display dates in Australian style (e.g. `Mon 19 Oct`). The user is an experienced boulderer (roughly V7+, working toward V8) and recreational cyclist who currently climbs at least twice a week and cycles 2–3 times per week, usually riding up to 40–60 km. The objective is to improve bouldering strength/technique, climbing power-endurance and cycling fitness over three weeks while managing fatigue. **Only a bouldering gym is available: do NOT include lead climbing, ropes, or route climbing.** The gym has ordinary boulders and a Kilter board.

## Weekly calendar and progression

| Day | Week 1 (19–25 Oct) | Week 2 (26 Oct–1 Nov) | Week 3 (2–8 Nov) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Hard bouldering/projecting, 90 min | Hard bouldering/projecting, 100–110 min | Hard bouldering/projecting, 75–90 min |

| Sunday | Rest, optional gentle yoga/walk | Rest, optional gentle yoga/walk | Rest, optional gentle yoga/walk |

The three-week progression is **establish workload → modest increase → consolidate/reduce load**. Keep one full rest day each week. Allow sessions to be skipped, shifted, or shortened as needed rather than treating every target as mandatory.

## Session library / instructions

### Monday: hard bouldering and projecting
- Warm up for ~20 min: mobility, easy boulders, progressively harder moves.
- Spend most of the session on difficult boulders or hard Kilter board projects at roughly RPE 8–9/10, with 3–5 min rest between serious attempts.
- Prioritise a small number of high-quality attempts and technique; stop if power or movement quality clearly deteriorates.
- Maximal hangboarding is **not** an additional required workout. If used at all, substitute for a portion of hard board climbing, rather than stacking both at maximal intensity.
- Weekly total duration as shown in calendar.

### Wednesday: bouldering endurance (choose ONE mode per session)
**Option A — 4×4 boulder circuits**
- After 15–20 min warming up, perform four consecutive manageable boulder problems as one circuit, with only ~15–30 seconds for changing problems and no long pauses. Rest 3–5 min between circuits.
- Choose problems well below maximal grade, approximately **3–4 V-grades below current limit** as an initial guide; adjust for gym grading and terrain so a circuit is hard but achievable with controlled movement.
- Week 1: **3 circuits**. Week 2: **4 circuits**. Week 3: **2–3 circuits**.
- Goal: meaningful forearm pump without repeatedly failing moves or sacrificing technique. Choose safe downclimbs; avoid rushing dangerous drops.

**Option B — Kilter board endurance links**
- After 15–20 min warming up, link easy moves or traverse on a Kilter board (as appropriate to the board layout and gym rules) at **moderate** intensity; use sufficiently positive holds and a comfortable angle.
- Week 1: **4 × 2 min** climbing, resting 2–3 min between efforts.
- Week 2: **5 × 2–3 min** climbing, resting 2–3 min between efforts.
- Week 3: **3 × 2 min** climbing, resting 2–3 min between efforts.
- Adjust difficulty, climb/rest duration or movement selection so technique stays controlled. Avoid desperate moves and repeated falls as fatigue builds. If continuous climbing is impractical, use linked easier boulders and short rests to achieve the same moderate pump.

Both options count as **the same Wednesday session**; they are alternatives, not extra training. Fill any remaining scheduled time with technique practice, easy climbing and recovery rather than adding maximum-intensity efforts. The displayed session description should let the user choose between the two methods.

### Friday: easy bouldering and technique
- Mostly comfortable boulders, roughly RPE 4–6/10; prioritise footwork, body positioning, pacing and enjoyment.
- Do not convert this into another max-project or high-pump session. On tired weeks, shorten or omit it.
- Optional 10–15 minutes of gentle shoulder, forearm and thoracic mobility.

### Tuesday: easy aerobic cycling + strength
- Zone 2 = conversational pace, about RPE 3–4/10. This is not an interval day.
- Ideally separate riding and strength by a few hours if convenient, but no need to make that mandatory.
- Strength workout (about 35 min in Weeks 1–2):
  - Bulgarian split squat: 2 sets × 8 reps/leg.
  - Romanian deadlift: 2 × 8.
  - Standing calf raises: 2 × 12.
  - Dumbbell bench press or push-ups: 2 × 8–12.
  - Chest-supported rows: 2 × 8.
  - Side plank: 2 × 30–45 seconds/side.
- Use manageable loads with about 2–3 reps in reserve. Do not exhaust the finger flexors or forearms before Wednesday's climbing endurance. In Week 3, reduce strength volume (e.g., one set per exercise), with no grinding reps.

### Thursday: cycling intervals
- 15 min easy warm-up; intervals at RPE **7–8/10**, not all-out sprints; 4 min easy cycling between efforts; 10 min easy cool-down.
- Week 1: **3 × 8 min**.
- Week 2: **3 × 10 min**.
- Week 3: **2 × 8 min**.
- Add easy cycling as needed to reach the approximate time ranges in the calendar.

### Saturday: long aerobic cycling
- Mostly Zone 2, conversational pace (RPE 3–4/10); no racing hills or chasing personal records.
- Week 1: **40–50 km**; Week 2: **50–60 km**; Week 3: **40–50 km**.
- For rides lasting longer than ~90 minutes, take water and suitable carbohydrates; aim for around 30–60 g of carbohydrate per hour according to tolerance, duration and intensity.

### Sunday and daily recovery
- Sunday is a full rest day, with an optional easy walk or restorative yoga for 20–30 min.
- Optional gentle mobility 10–15 min on most days: thoracic rotation, shoulder movement, hip flexors, calves/ankles. Do not turn stretching into another intense workout.
- If sleep is poor, fatigue is high, or fingers/elbows hurt: reduce volume or replace a hard session with rest/easy training. Increasing finger/elbow pain is a reason to stop provocative climbing and reassess; avoid max hangs on sore tendons.

## Website requirements

Build a clean, mobile-first training dashboard using the website's existing styles:
1. Header: **“3-Week Climbing & Cycling Block”**, dates **19 Oct – 8 Nov 2026**, and a short description: “Three bouldering sessions, three rides and one strength session each week, balanced with recovery.”
2. Display the plan in a **three-week calendar or tabbed week view**, with seven day cards in Monday–Sunday order, dates, activities, durations/distance, workout type and intensity.
3. Use visually distinct but accessible labels for **Bouldering**, **Cycling**, **Strength**, **Recovery**. Do not rely on color alone.
4. Each day expands to show its full session details, sets/reps, pacing, and safety/recovery instructions; use the session library above as the source.
5. Make Wednesdays explicitly selectable between **4×4 circuits** and **Kilter endurance**. Persist this choice locally if persistence is supported.
6. Include **completion checkboxes per activity** (Tuesday has separate cycling and strength activities) and a small **notes** field for each day or workout. Persist user progress using browser localStorage unless the existing application already has a more appropriate persistence mechanism. Avoid requiring a backend.
7. Offer lightweight weekly progress summaries, e.g. number of climbing/riding/strength sessions completed versus planned. Show Week 1/2/3 phase labels **Build**, **Progress**, **Consolidate**.
8. Optional logging fields, if easy to add: session RPE, ride km, climbing grade, fatigue or finger/elbow discomfort. These start blank; do **not** invent workout results.
9. Keep training prescription separate from completion/log data. Use accessible controls, readable typography, responsive spacing, and support keyboard interactions.
10. Ensure all calendar dates are explicit and local to **Australia/Perth** (avoid date shifting from UTC parsing); do not dynamically roll the plan forward from the current date.
