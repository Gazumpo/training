# Gym exercise review

Reviewed 21 October 2026 against the goals of improving climbing and cycling, supporting injury prevention, and building useful general strength. Equipment: dumbbells, kettlebell, pull-up bar, floor space and a stable flat bench. No adjustable incline, barbell, rack, machines, strength measurements or current injury diagnosis are assumed. A concern about back injury was raised; current back pain was not reported.

The week has three bouldering sessions, three rides and one supporting gym session. Tuesday falls between Monday hard bouldering and Wednesday endurance climbing. Exercise selection therefore has to account for forearm, shoulder and whole-body recovery.

## Recommended session

Keep five main movements: standard dumbbell split squat, Romanian deadlift, dumbbell bench press, side plank and hanging knee / leg raise. The user reports dead bugs and bird dogs are easy for 50 reps, so they remain easier alternatives rather than the main abdominal stimulus. Use one exercise per slot; library alternatives do not add more required work. Follow strength with light side-lying external rotation and scapular push-ups, repeated after Friday climbing. Calf raises and rows remain optional.

The clear additional swap enabled by the bench is the optional row: replace thigh support with hand-and-knee support on the bench. This is a practical setup decision, not a claim of superior climbing transfer or lower injury rates. Its existing exercise ID, selection and saved load remain intact; reassess the working load for the new setup. Instructions, photos and fallback diagram now show bench support.

## Review of every library exercise

| Exercise | Decision | Reason and use |
|---|---|---|
| Dumbbell split squat | Keep as the main leg choice | Loadable knee-and-hip strength with both feet on the floor. Light hand support is acceptable if balance limits the legs. A bench does not make rear-foot elevation necessary. Normal: 2 × 6-8 per leg. |
| Romanian deadlift | Keep as the main hip hinge | Adds hip-extension and hamstring loading alongside the knee-dominant leg exercise. Start light and progress within a comfortable range. Stop and reassess if it causes back pain. Normal: 2 × 6-8. |
| Dumbbell bench press | Keep as the main push | Straightforward progressive chest/triceps strength that complements climbing. Use a comfortable shoulder range. It does not, by itself, establish balanced shoulders or prevent injury. Normal: 2 × 8-12. |
| Side plank | Keep as the lateral core choice | Useful general trunk strength with little finger demand. Progress difficulty after controlled 40-second holds rather than making holds indefinitely longer. Normal: 2 × 20-40 seconds per side. |
| Hanging knee / leg raise | Replace dead bug as the main abdominal choice | User-approved progression given the reported ability and available pull-up bar. Start 2 × 6-10 controlled reps after a one-set introduction, with 90 seconds rest and about 2 reps in reserve. Bend knees initially, include a gentle pelvic curl, lower for 2-3 seconds, and progressively straighten legs once 10 strict reps are comfortable. Use reverse crunches if hanging causes pain, grip limits the set, or recovery suffers. Direct superiority for climbing performance is unproven. |
| Dead bug | Keep as an easier alternative | Useful for abdominal control or warm-up, but the current version is too easy to remain this user's main strength stimulus. |
| Side-lying external rotation | Keep in Tuesday/Friday shoulder care | Low-load external-rotator conditioning with little grip demand. The floor remains suitable; a bench is not needed to improve it. Start 1 × 12-15 per side, build to 2 sets when recovered. |
| Scapular push-up | Keep; add bench incline as a regression | Trains serratus and shoulder-blade control. Use hands on a stable bench, a wall, knees or floor according to ability. Leave several reps available. Start 1 × 8-12, build to 2 sets. |
| Standing calf raise | Keep optional | General calf/ankle capacity. Its independent cycling or climbing benefit is uncertain, so it remains lower priority than the main leg exercises. Optional 2 × 12. |
| Bench-supported dumbbell row | Swap from thigh-supported row; keep optional | A hand and same-side knee on the bench give a steadier base. Useful horizontal pulling if recovered or addressing a weakness. Climbing three times weekly already supplies substantial pulling and finger load. Optional 2 × 8 per side, about 3 reps in reserve. |
| Goblet squat | Keep as a leg alternative | Useful leg strength, but no essential additional benefit when split squats already occupy this slot. Start unloaded or light and use a comfortable depth. Choose split squats if preferred; do not perform both as required work. Alternative 2 × 8-10 after a one-set introduction. |
| Reverse lunge | Keep as a leg alternative | Useful if stepping feels better than a static stance. Adds a moving balance demand, with no established advantage over split squats for this plan. |
| Glute bridge | Keep as a hip-extension alternative | Useful when RDL grip fatigue interferes with climbing, or when a bridge is more comfortable. It is not equivalent hamstring training. Load it progressively or use a controlled single-leg version when easy. |
| Push-up | Keep as a push alternative | General pushing and trunk strength. The bench now offers a convenient incline regression. Choose it instead of bench press if preferred and comfortable. |
| Dumbbell overhead press | Keep as a push alternative, not an extra | Useful general overhead strength if tolerated. No clear reason to displace the requested bench press or add more pressing volume solely for injury prevention. |
| Controlled pull-up | Keep optional | Useful for a demonstrated vertical-pulling strength limitation. It can be valuable for climbing, but adding it between climbing days without a clear need may cost recovery. Use submaximal sets or the row when appropriate. |
| Scapular pull-up | Keep as an optional pulling alternative | Small-range hanging shoulder control. It is not a complete substitute for progressive pulling strength or the shoulder routine. Omit if hanging is uncomfortable or fingers are fatigued. |
| Bird dog | Keep as an easier core alternative | Useful for practising coordinated trunk control. Often too light to remain the main strength stimulus once easy; retain the side plank or progress difficulty when able. |
| Suitcase carry | Keep as a core alternative | Loadable trunk work, but it adds grip demand. Prefer side plank in this particular Tuesday slot if the carry affects Wednesday climbing. |
| Reverse crunch | Keep as an abdominal alternative | Dynamic abdominal work without hanging from tired fingers. Choose instead of hanging raises when grip or shoulders need a break, rather than adding another core exercise. |
| Supported wrist extension | Keep optional; add bench forearm support | The bench provides a stable forearm rest, although the thigh is also suitable. Light extensor-capacity work is reasonable when tolerated; it is not a universal treatment or prevention prescription for climber elbow. Start with one set. |

## Bench exercises considered without adding them

- **Bulgarian split squat:** reasonable later if comfortable and preferred. More rear-foot elevation, balance demand and range are not automatically better for cycling or climbing. Keep the standard split squat as the repeatable starting choice.
- **Bench hip thrust:** a possible later hip-extension alternative if bridges become difficult to load sufficiently. It does not replace the RDL's hamstring stimulus, and no evidence reviewed establishes that it would improve this combined programme more than the present choices.
- **Chest-supported row:** reasonable if the bench has a suitable incline and allows the dumbbells to move freely. Only a flat bench is confirmed, so use the supported one-arm row.
- **Bench step-up:** requires a platform of suitable height and stability, designed to take stepping loads. A weight bench alone does not establish suitability; no need to replace a loadable split squat.
- **Bench dips:** no clear additional benefit for the goals once bench press and shoulder conditioning are present, so no extra pressing exercise is added.

## Loading, recovery and injury prevention

Use two working sets for main strength work, or one with Lighter. Start unfamiliar exercises with one set for the first week. Rest 2-3 minutes for loaded legs/hinges, 60-90 seconds for pushing and core, and 45-60 seconds for shoulder work. Take longer if needed to maintain control. Keep 2-3 good reps in reserve for strength and at least 3-4 for shoulder care.

When both sets reach the top of the rep range with consistent technique and the intended reserve on two sessions, increase by the smallest practical load and return to the lower end. Saved loads never increase automatically. General strength exercises must become challenging enough to progress, but this is not a prescription to attempt maximal lifts or force a painful range.

Allow approximately 40-50 minutes for the main workout and shoulder care, or 25-35 with Lighter. Optional work adds time. If Tuesday compromises Wednesday climbing or Thursday riding, omit optional pulling/calves first, then reduce sets or load. New leg exercises can create soreness, so introduce changes gradually.

Once-weekly strength is a practical starting dose in this full schedule. Cycling studies support progressive resistance training, but differ in equipment, load and frequency; the best dose for this exact combination is uncertain. One session can help, and there is no evidence-based requirement to add a second session before assessing progress and recovery. If strength stalls despite consistent progressive training and good recovery, reconsider dose or scheduling rather than adding numerous accessories.

The shoulder exercises are sensible capacity work, not a guarantee against climbing injuries. Conditioning, managing effort and recovery, movement technique, and responding to persistent symptoms all matter. Neither a bench nor a specific exercise is inherently protective. No generic accessory programme can be tailored to an unknown injury history. Persistent or recurring pain needs individual assessment.

Climbing practice remains the most specific climbing work. Targeted finger or pulling training can improve relevant capacities, but no current assessment establishes that more is needed here. Do not automatically add maximal hangboarding or campus work to an already full climbing week. Hanging abdominal work now replaces the easier abdominal exercise; it is not an additional core slot. If a limitation is identified, substitute targeted training for some existing load and assess the response.

## Evidence and limits

These choices combine published evidence with practical programming judgement. No study directly tests this exact home programme against the proposed bench alternatives.

- [2025 cycling heavy-strength meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632222/). Seventeen studies found improvements in cycling efficiency, anaerobic power and performance; certainty was low, and optimal implementation remains uncertain. This does not prove a particular dumbbell exercise or weekly dose is best.
- [Resistance Training for Cyclists, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42828469/). A recent narrative review supports moderate-to-high-load lower-body work with limited fatigue, commonly 1-2 weekly sessions. It informs the emphasis on progression and recovery, rather than requiring this exact prescription.
- [Ronnestad et al., 2015: cycling strength trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24862305/). A small elite-cyclist trial supports adding heavy strength work over months; equipment and protocol differ from this plan.
- [Stien et al., 2023: climbing-training systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36636194/). Specific resistance training improved climbing-related test outcomes, particularly finger capacities. Small studies and heterogeneous outcomes limit exact prescriptions and claims about climbing-grade improvement.
- [Saeterbakken et al., 2018: climber core-training trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30303973/). Nineteen climbers trained dynamic or isometric core twice weekly for ten weeks. Some test outcomes improved; neither approach was clearly superior. No inactive control and no direct test of this hanging-raise/side-plank pair.
- [Kozin et al., 2021: climber shoulder-prevention trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34846050/). A broader programme reduced reported injuries in young male amateurs. That does not validate the exact two shoulder exercises or dose here.
- [2023 climber overuse-injury review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38162697/). Supports considering conditioning, load management and technique, while highlighting limited prevention evidence. Associations with injury are not proof of causation.
- [Decker et al., 1999: serratus activation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10569366/). Supports the muscle target of push-up-plus variations, not a guarantee of prevention.
- [ACE: single-arm row](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/126/single-arm-row/), [chest press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/19/chest-press/) and [goblet squat](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/362/goblet-squat/). Technique references, not comparative performance or injury trials.
- [Guy's and St Thomas': low-back-pain exercise guidance](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/low-back-pain/physiotherapy-and-exercises). Supports staying active and individual advice when needed; it does not establish suitability of loaded squats or hinges for every back condition.

- [Saint Paul Fire Department exercise appendix, pages 8-9](https://www.stpaul.gov/sites/default/files/Media%20Root/Human%20Resources/Preparing%20for%20the%20SPFD%20Physical%20Performance%20Test%20Download%20Part%204%20-%20Exercise%20Appendix.pdf#page=8). Illustrates hanging knee and straight-leg raise variations; technique reference, not a climbing-performance trial. The exact dose is a practical starting prescription.
